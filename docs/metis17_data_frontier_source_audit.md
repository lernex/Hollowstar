# Metis-1.7: dated frontier data evidence

Research cutoff: **2026-09-04**. Scope: text/code acquisition and the
30T TST + 5T NTP brief. This is supporting evidence for the
[acquisition plan](metis17_200tb_pretraining_corpus_research.md), not a source
lock or an assertion that a publisher's tokens equal Metis tokens.

**Operator revision, September 4:** the active plan acquires complete
Nemotron-CC v2, v2.1 and CC-Math payloads; removes bulk CC URL indexes,
indexed WARC repair, the GitHub archive lane and repository-dependent
replay; and requires preparation concurrent with download. The
[1.7 prep contract](metis17_data_prep_plan.md) applies the 1.6 lessons.
Research below describing excluded options is evidence, not authorization
to restore those acquisition paths.

Evidence levels used here:

- **Inspected primary evidence:** an original report, official release/card,
  or publisher API/manifest was opened.
- **Publisher-reported quantity:** a claim by the publisher, not independently
  measured with the Metis tokenizer.
- **Planning allocation:** an engineering choice, not an available-payload
  measurement or a recommended training percentage.
- **Unresolved:** insufficient evidence, access, licensing, or measurement.

Release date, metadata update date, crawl date, and document publication date
are different. A September 2026 repository update does not make its contents
September 2026 knowledge. Search-engine summaries were used for discovery,
not as evidence: several invented corpus cutoffs, released curation code, and
even quotations that were absent from the underlying reports.

### 1.1 TST: promising evidence, not a 35T quality warranty

The May 2026 Nous result reports 4,768 versus 12,311 B200-hours and losses
2.236 versus 2.252 for the TST and baseline 10B-A1B runs. Those are measurements
of that configuration. They do not imply that every 2.28 superposition tokens
always buy one ordinary-NTP token of quality, or that 30T TST followed by 5T
NTP is equivalent to a particular much larger conventional run.

The author's May 2026 explanation explicitly identifies data-constrained
training as a limitation: full TST consumes more source tokens, and its
output-only alternative is a research option. It also states that a useful
scaling law and downstream long-context benefits have not been established.
The dated follow-up search found no decision-grade published validation at
the proposed 35T scale; that is a search finding, not proof none exists.

Code, mathematics and proofs must receive ordinary causal training. A
conservative TST view can initially emphasize prose, but the claim that code
or math *cannot* benefit from any TST exposure is also unproven. Both a
same-mixture control and the proposed broad-to-premium transition need a
Metis canary. A damaged TST initialization is not guaranteed to become the
same model as an independently trained 5T NTP baseline.

The [May 2026 revised PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06546v2), inspected
directly, supplies a particularly important counterexample in Table 2:
reinitializing input embeddings and the output head at the phase boundary
produces final loss **2.938**, versus **2.808** for the matched-step baseline
and **2.676** for ordinary TST. Preserve representation continuity; the
recovery phase is not a proven guarantee against every bad first phase.

### 1.2 Repetition: neither a universal ban nor a free multiplier

| Primary source and date | Actual scope | What can be used | What must not be inferred |
|---|---|---|---|

| [Scaling Domain Data Repetition in LLM Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14071), August 2026 | Primary abstract: at fixed tokens/parameter, repetition optima vary by model and domain; unique domain size alone is a weak predictor | Calibrate replay on matched-regime proxies and domain validation, rather than an arbitrary corpus-wide limit | Full HTML was unavailable; no uninspected detailed result is used, and no TST extrapolation is warranted |
| [Replaying pre-training data improves fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04964), March 2026 | Controlled 150M runs with 4M target / 4B total tokens; practical 8B fine-tuning examples | Broad-data replay can help target learning, not only prevent generic forgetting | Its 1.87x fine-tuning / 2.06x mid-training data-efficiency results do not prescribe a Metis recovery percentage |

The first study's generic stream stays fresh while a scarce domain repeats.
That distinction is essential. Replaying a complete stale corpus and mixing
limited premium data into fresh broad text are not the same experiment.

## 2. Consensus, contested choices, and practices to retire

**Convergent evidence in the inspected late-2025/2026 sources:** staged
training; richer code and scientific documents; preserving document and
repository structure; multiple quality signals; explicit source/phase
accounting; and evaluating downstream capability as well as loss.

**Contested, not consensus:** aggressive synthetic enrichment versus excluding
synthetic math/science, exact blend percentages, replay limits, cross-snapshot
near-deduplication scope, and transfer of full TST to a 35T hybrid-model run.
For example, GLM-5's February 2026 math/science collection explicitly filters
synthetic and templated content. That is not compatible with calling
synthetic superiority a universal frontier result.

**Retire from this acquisition plan:**

- Treating 200 TB compressed as 200 TB decoded text.
- Treating 35T exposures as a mandatory 42-45T unique-token acquisition gate.
- Treating the number of dataset names as independent information coverage.
- Using a new release date as proof of new underlying documents.
- Buying every overlapping parent and educational/rewrite child without a
  physical-payload and marginal-coverage audit.
- Applying one educational score or legacy C4/Gopher/KenLM gate to all news,
  code, scientific writing and low-resource languages.
- Claiming that TST allows benchmark-contamination or privacy safeguards to
  be relaxed.
- Promising ten days from two nominal 1GbE links without measured routing,
  publisher throughput, and acquisition-origin balance.

### 2.1 Recent extraction and deduplication research

| Primary source and date | Reported result | Decision boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [Beyond a Single Extractor](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19548v1), February 2026; [EACL publication](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.307/), March 2026 | Combining selected pages from different extractors increased yield up to 71%; a second fuzzy-dedup pass removed 7-8%, still leaving up to 58% more tokens. Standard-task differences include single-run noise | Extraction changes coverage. Compare WET against faithful HTML extraction on a bounded pilot; do not assume every page needs multiple stored renderings |
| [Scalable Frequency- and Length-Aware Subdocument Deduplication](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03089v1), August 2026 preprint | Separates global duplicate detection from copy retention; 294.9B-token runs; reported FineWeb-Edu aggregate 51.89 baseline, 51.51 with document MinHash, 52.90 combined method | Global accounting and global deletion are different. Keep coherent unique material and explicitly measure frequency/length-aware retention; this is not a universal 35T recipe |
| [Understanding Data Temporality Impact on Large Language Models Pre-training](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22769v2), May 2026; submitted May 21, revised May 25 | 6B models and a 2.5T-token baseline; temporal QA evaluation and sequential-snapshot training. A 2024-data cooldown on a 2021 sequential checkpoint improves reported OLMES by 1.5 points | Keep capture cohorts and test a recency-aware ordinary-NTP tail. The shuffled baseline covers 2020-2024 while the sequential run covers 2018-2025: this is not a clean same-data proof that ordering alone causes fresher knowledge |

The first paper's union is over high-quality pages and resolves page
identity; it is not evidence for counting alternative extractions of the
same page as independent facts. The second warns that accidental shard-
local visibility makes retention depend on partition layout. Bounded local
execution state must not accidentally define corpus-wide duplicate policy.

### 3.1 Benchmark ancestry can block apparently valid training subsets

Inspected September 2026:
[the registry](../manifests/contamination/eval-holdouts.yaml) pins specific
configuration/split jobs. GPQA, IFEval and some competition sets use splits
called `train` while remaining evaluation-only. GSM8K, MATH and SWE-bench
are configured with test splits; the registry does not literally index
every training split.

Separately,
[`benchmark_genealogy_aliases` and `benchmark_genealogy_match`](../src/metis_data/decontaminate.py)
construct benchmark/repository aliases without a split exception and inspect
declared seed/source-dataset metadata. Consequently, a synthetic collection
with explicit benchmark ancestry can be rejected even when it used training
seeds. A publisher's `train` label therefore cannot substitute for the Metis
genealogy gate. Any different 1.7 policy must be a deliberate new-release
decision, not an accidental relaxation to admit more premium tokens.

### 3.2 Existing resolver behavior versus the new budget contract

Inspected September 2026:
[`source_lock._iter_repo_files`](../src/metis_data/source_lock.py) already
uses `HfApi.list_repo_tree`, pinned revisions, minimal literal roots,
`recursive=True`, `expand=False`, and allow/deny patterns. It collects
size/blob/LFS-hash evidence and deduplicates paths. The research-ledger
truncation must not be incorrectly attributed to that existing iterator.
Use the existing mechanism and add independent inventory reconciliation.

The same file's `_select_files` is intentionally target-filling: it appends
objects until the total reaches or exceeds `target_bytes`; `take_all=True`
returns every matching object. This is not a strict maximum-transfer
selector. The new CSV's ceilings, quality/language strata and acquisition-
batch composition need an explicit 1.7 contract rather than being silently
mapped to those 1.6 target semantics.

### 4.2 Investigated index repair, excluded from the active plan

No bulk URL-index or WET-to-WARC lookup is funded in the revised plan.
The following explains the dependency that was investigated and then
removed; retaining parent IDs remains useful provenance without retrieval.

The [July 2026 announcement](https://commoncrawl.org/blog/july-2026-crawl-archive-now-available)
adds UUIDv7 response IDs, CDXJ `recordid`, and Parquet `warc_record_id`; it also
switches recent Parquet indexes to Zstandard. The
[July 2026 schema](https://github.com/commoncrawl/cc-index-table/blob/10c824e7df0402c6d76e7aea085d18b2134f2625/src/main/resources/schema/cc-index-schema-flat.json#L257-L295)
dates the record-ID field to `CC-MAIN-2026-30`.

WET's conversion record has its own ID and refers to the original response
through `WARC-Refers-To`; the latter is a parent UUID, not a compressed
offset. WET and WARC payload digests are also not interchangeable. Matching
July/August parents to ranges would require the columnar index; earlier
snapshots need another locator path. Those dependencies are exactly what
the current no-reconstruction plan avoids.

The [Datatrove WARC reader](https://github.com/huggingface/datatrove/blob/a649de79c14a550dc90f48a15c025f2dd3fd3b57/src/datatrove/pipeline/readers/warc.py#L87-L140),
inspected at an August 2026 commit, emits text/id/url/date but drops
`WARC-Refers-To`. Preserve it in the reader before downstream adapters.
Its [rank completion markers](https://github.com/huggingface/datatrove/blob/a649de79c14a550dc90f48a15c025f2dd3fd3b57/src/datatrove/executor/base.py#L156-L177)
are not a replacement for manifest-bound object receipts; reusing a
completion directory after changing rank assignments can skip different work.

These are research findings, not deployed modifications. Use the
[official access guidance](https://commoncrawl.org/get-started) for selected
whole objects. The [URL-index guidance](https://commoncrawl.org/url-index)
is not an active acquisition dependency. Do not infer AWS billing terms
from a third-party example.
Common Crawl's compilation does not waive underlying third-party rights;
inspect the current [terms](https://commoncrawl.org/terms-of-use) and
[opt-out registry](https://commoncrawl.org/blog/common-crawl-foundation-opt-out-registry).

### 4.3 Freshness needs a capability measure, not just timestamp counts

Kyutai's May 2026 temporality study releases
[Kairos code](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/kairos) and
[KairosQA/checkpoints](https://huggingface.co/collections/kyutai/kairos).
The benchmark targets changing facts through 2025, with a substantial
sports/awards concentration. It is a useful **evaluation candidate**, not
pretraining data or a complete test of September 2026 knowledge.

For Metis, use held-out temporal probes that distinguish current answers,
historically correct answers and unsupported dates; separately cover
software/API changes and 2026 world knowledge. Freeze evaluation prompts
and synthetic descendants outside training. Do not import Kairos's exact
filter thresholds or promote its temporally asymmetric comparison into a
guaranteed TST schedule improvement.

- **46,445,603,916 compressed payload bytes**, approximately **0.046446 TB**.

  `0fabd2eddae6631957481391c5613fbea3a39801c61475ce5a3be1e30e6bff07`.
- Largest object approximately 2.809 GB; the files contain current revision
  content, not repository pointers or an all-history dump.

This is a strong small reference-refresh candidate within a **0.05 TB**

The [official licensing guide](https://dumps.wikimedia.org/legal.html)
identifies CC BY-SA 4.0/GFDL text terms and exceptions. Preserve attribution
and review redistribution/model-release obligations under the chosen
policy. This is not a claim that every revision is error-free, free of
third-party exceptions, or newly written in September; actual revision-date
and temporal novelty fields remain necessary.

### 5.1 The overlooked Ultra-FineWeb branches are real payload

### 6.4 arXiv source text: preserve what exists, do not overstate fidelity

The [dataset license](https://huggingface.co/datasets/scholarweave/arxiv-latex/blob/a64471103c2563f6428e61ba7ab28b33417a47a5/LICENSE)

does not turn an arXiv distribution grant into universal downstream rights.

The [July 2026 ETL implementation](https://github.com/arthiondaena/arxivETL_sync/blob/3c7f8821104ce63c084ee34681f0d0c0e3bd8187/arxiv_etl_rust/src/lib.rs)

sorts filenames and joins with `FILE:` boundaries. It also has a raw-text
fallback. The result is **not a byte-exact complete source archive** and
does not establish successful LaTeX compilation. Preserve macros and file
boundaries; check replacement characters, malformed/null content and version
associations before admission. Whole-paper KaTeX rendering is not a gate.
